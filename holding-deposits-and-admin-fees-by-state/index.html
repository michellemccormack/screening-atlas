Holding Deposits and "Admin Fees" vs Application Fees: Which States Regulate Each

Application fees get most of the attention, but applicants also run into "holding deposits," "admin fees," "processing fees" and "move-in fees." States that regulate these usually do it in different sections from the application-fee cap, so a fee that's fine under one rule can still be barred by another. This page covers the non-screening charges; for the screening or application fee itself, see application fee caps by state.

The short version. Washington caps a holding deposit at 25% of the first month's rent, requires a receipt and written conditions, and bans waiting-list fees [RCW 59.18.253]. Oregon bans applicant deposits and fees other than the screening charge, except a deposit to secure a rental agreement taken after you approve the applicant [ORS 90.297]. California counts fees "to reimburse the landlord for costs associated with processing a new tenant" as security, subject to the deposit cap [Civ. Code 1950.5(b)]. Massachusetts and Hawaii list the only amounts you may collect at the start of a tenancy [MA] [HI]. New York limits pre-lease screening charges to $20 and bars "key money" [NY AG guide].

The $75 + $150 + $45 example

Take an applicant charged a $75 application fee, a $150 admin fee and a $45 screening fee. Under the rules above:

  • Oregon: only the screening charge allowed by ORS 90.295 may be collected from an applicant, plus a post-approval deposit to secure the agreement [ORS 90.297(1)]. A separate admin fee charged to an applicant doesn't fit either category.
  • California: an admin or processing fee imposed at the start of the tenancy counts toward the security deposit cap [1950.5(b)].
  • Massachusetts and Hawaii: neither appears on the statutory list of what may be collected at the start [MA] [HI].
  • New York: pre-lease screening charges top out at $20 [NY AG guide].
