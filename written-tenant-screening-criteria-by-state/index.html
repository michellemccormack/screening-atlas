Written Tenant Screening Criteria: Where You Must Give Applicants Your Rules Before Charging a Fee

"Write your screening rules down before you list" is common landlord advice. In some states it's also the law: if you haven't given the applicant your written criteria first, you may not be allowed to charge a screening fee at all, and getting it wrong can cost you the fee plus a penalty. Here is what four state statutes require, when, and what happens if you skip it.

The short version. Oregon: adopt written criteria and give a written notice covering nine items before accepting any screening charge [ORS 90.295(3)]. Washington: before obtaining any information about the applicant, notify them in writing or by posting what you'll check and what can lead to denial [RCW 59.18.257(1)(a)]. Minnesota: disclose in writing, before accepting a screening fee, the screening service you use and "the criteria on which the decision to rent" will be based [504B.173 subd. 3]. California: under the first-come screening option, give the criteria in writing "together with the application form" [Civ. Code 1950.6(c)(2)(A)(i)].

After the decision: the criteria come back

Written criteria matter again when you say no. In Minnesota, you must notify the applicant within 14 days of rejecting the application, identifying the criteria they failed to meet [504B.173 subd. 3(2)]. In Washington, an adverse action notice must state the reasons, using the statutory form [59.18.257(1)(c)]. Oregon's denial-notice rule and the federal adverse action requirements are covered on our adverse action notice page and the Oregon screening rules page.

What a working criteria sheet usually lists

Taking the four statutes together, a single sheet that would cover each one's required topics names: the screening company (with address and phone, for Minnesota), the kinds of records you check, the specific results that lead to denial, the fee, whether you accept a reusable report (Washington), the applicant's dispute and appeal rights, your nondiscrimination policy, and the rent and deposits (Oregon) [OR] [WA] [MN] [CA]. Some cities add their own requirements on top; this page covers state law only.
