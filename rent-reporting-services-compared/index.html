Rent Reporting to Credit Bureaus: TurboTenant, RentRedi, Avail and Innago Compared

A renter with years of on-time payments can still have a thin credit file, because rent usually isn't reported. Most landlord rent-collection apps now sell an add-on that reports it. They differ on which bureaus get the data, whether past months can be added, what it costs and whether the landlord can pay. In California, some landlords must offer it.

The short version. RentRedi reports to all three bureaus for $3.99–$5.99 a month [RentRedi]. Innago reports to TransUnion free, or to all three for $5.99 a month [Innago]. TurboTenant reports to TransUnion and Experian for $4.99 a month [TurboTenant]. Avail reports to TransUnion at $3.95 per reported month [Avail]. All four describe reporting on-time (positive) payments; none of them reports late rent through these products.

California: larger landlords must offer it

Since January 1, 2025, California requires landlords to offer each tenant on the lease the option of having positive rental payment information reported to at least one nationwide consumer reporting agency [Civ. Code 1954.07(a)]. Key details:

  • Timing: for leases signed on or after April 1, 2025, at signing and at least once a year after; for leases outstanding on January 1, 2025, by April 1, 2025 and yearly after [(b)].
  • The written offer must say reporting is optional, name each bureau, state any fee, explain how to opt in and out, and note that a tenant who opts out can't resume for six months [(d)].
  • Fee: the lesser of the landlord's actual cost or $10 a month, and nothing if the landlord has no cost. Nonpayment can't be reported, can't be grounds for eviction and can't be taken from the deposit [(g), (h)].
  • Exemptions: buildings with 15 or fewer units, unless the landlord owns more than one rental building and is a REIT, a corporation, or an LLC with at least one corporate member; and assisted housing developments [(j)].

Does reported rent help an applicant you're screening?

Rent reported this way becomes part of the applicant's credit file at the bureau that receives it; RentRedi says TransUnion recommends 12 months of on-time payments "for the greatest potential impact" [source].
