Is the Previous Landlord Real? Checking Property Records Before a Reference Call

The landlord reference sounds great, and the phone number on the application goes to someone who says all the right things. But is that person the owner of the applicant's last address, or a friend? Before the reference call, you can check who owns the property in the local government's own records. Most counties and big cities have a free online lookup.

The short version. Search the applicant's previous address in the county assessor's or recorder's database, note the owner of record, and compare it with the name the "landlord" gave you. Then call the owner or manager using contact information you found yourself, not just the number on the application. A mismatch isn't proof of fraud, since the owner may be an LLC or use a property manager, but it's a reason to ask more questions.

Where to look up who owns an address

Two kinds of local offices keep property records. The assessor (or tax office) values property for taxes. The recorder (or register of deeds, or county clerk) records deeds and other documents. Examples of official lookups:

Elsewhere, search for your county's assessor or recorder and make sure the site ends in .gov or is linked from the county's official site.

How to check a previous landlord, step by step

  1. Take the exact previous address from the application, including the unit number.
  2. Look it up in the county's assessor or recorder database and write down the owner of record.
  3. Compare that owner with the reference's name. If the owner is a company, check whether the reference works for it or for a property manager.
  4. Find the owner's or manager's contact details on your own, and call that number.
  5. Ask questions only the real landlord could answer, such as the monthly rent and the dates of the tenancy, and compare the answers with the application.
  6. Write down what you found and keep it with the application file.

What if the owner is an LLC?

Many rentals are owned by a company, so the record may not show a person's name. Search the company in the state's business-entity database to see its registered agent and filing details. Our guide to checking an employer in state business records lists official state searches, and the same searches work for a landlord's LLC.

Does checking ownership change your FCRA duties?

Looking up public property records yourself isn't buying a report from a screening company. A reference call is different, and if you pay a company to do the calls, the rules change.
