Can a Landlord Take Rent Up Front? Prepaid Rent Limits in CA, MA, NY, HI and OR

A common offer from applicants with low scores, no U.S. credit history or a recent job change: "I'll pay six months up front." Whether a landlord can take that money depends on the state, because many states count advance rent toward the security deposit limit or cap everything you may collect before move-in. Here are five states we've checked against the statute or the state's official guide.

The short version. California: advance rent counts as "security" (capped at one month's rent, or two for some small landlords), except that an advance payment of at least six months' rent is allowed on a lease of six months or longer [Civ. Code 1950.5]. Massachusetts: at or before the start of a tenancy you may collect no more than first month, last month, a security deposit equal to one month and the cost of a lock and key [G.L. c.186 §15B(1)(b)]. New York: the security deposit is limited to one month's rent, so a landlord "cannot ask for last month's rent and a security deposit" [NY AG guide]. Hawaii: at the start, only the first month's rent and the security deposit [HRS 521-44(b)]. Oregon: a last month's rent deposit is a security deposit, and unused prepaid rent must be accounted for and refunded [ORS 90.300].

Details that trip landlords up

  • Massachusetts last month's rent earns interest. If you take last month's rent in advance, give a receipt with the details listed in the statute and pay the tenant interest at 5% a year (or the lesser amount the bank pays) [§15B(2)(a)].
  • Oregon last month's rent can't be redirected. Unless you both agree, the tenant can't make you apply a last month's rent deposit to any month other than the last, and that deposit "does not limit the amount of rent charged" unless a written agreement says so [90.300(10)].
  • Hawaii deposits aren't last month's rent unless you and the tenant agree in writing and the tenant gives 45 days' notice of leaving [HRS 521-44(b)].
  • California landlords can't call any of it "nonrefundable." A lease may not describe any security as nonrefundable [1950.5(n)].

Prepaid rent isn't a substitute for screening

Accepting money up front doesn't change your other obligations. If a screening report led you to require extra money (for example, a larger deposit or last month's rent) rather than approve on standard terms, that can be an adverse action that needs a notice; see adverse action notices and conditional approvals after a credit check.
