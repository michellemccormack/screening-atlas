Where to Look Up Eviction Records: Official Court Portals by State

A screening report's eviction section is only as good as the court data behind it, and some providers say their eviction data is thin or missing in whole states. The court's own portal is the primary source. This page lists official portals we've confirmed, what each says it covers, and the state laws that keep some eviction cases out of view.

Before you search: a name match isn't proof it's the same person; check addresses and dates. And a record you can find may be one you're not allowed to use. Oregon, for example, bars landlords from considering dismissed evictions and judgments five or more years old [ORS 90.303]. If you deny someone based on a consumer report, federal adverse-action rules apply.

Why the court portal matters

TransUnion SmartMove's disclaimer (revised August 12, 2026) says eviction records are not available in Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, South Dakota and Wyoming, and are limited in 19 more states, including California, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Washington and Wisconsin [SmartMove]. If your report shows "no eviction records" for an applicant from one of those states, that may mean "no data," not "no evictions."

Where eviction records are hidden or limited by law

StateRuleSource
CaliforniaUnlawful detainer (eviction) court records are restricted. The public can see them only in listed cases, for example 60 days after filing if the landlord won a judgment against all defendants within those 60 days, or by court order. Before that, access is limited to the parties, residents of the unit, and people who give the clerk a plaintiff's and defendant's names plus the full address [source]Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161.2
MinnesotaCourts must expunge an eviction case if the tenant prevailed, the complaint was dismissed, the parties agreed, or three years have passed since the eviction was ordered, among other grounds [source]Minn. Stat. § 484.014
WashingtonLimited dissemination orders (for example where the landlord's case lacked basis or the tenancy was reinstated) bar screening companies from reporting the case or using it in a score [source]RCW 59.18.367
WisconsinMinimum time on the public site: 2 years for dismissed evictions with no money judgment, 10 years with a writ of restitution [source]Wis. Stat. § 758.20
OregonThe record may be public, but landlords may not consider dismissed cases, judgments for the applicant, judgments 5+ years old, or judgments on claims arising April 1, 2020 to March 1, 2022 [source]ORS 90.303; see our Oregon screening guide
