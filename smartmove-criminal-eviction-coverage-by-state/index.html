SmartMove Coverage by State: Where Criminal and Eviction Records Can Come Back

TransUnion sells SmartMoveaffiliate link nationwide, but its own disclaimer says criminal records can only be returned in 30 states, and that its eviction report is unavailable in five states and limited in parts of nineteen more [SmartMove disclaimer]. This page puts those lists into one table so you can check your rental's state before you order a report or charge an applicant for one.

The short version. Criminal records: available to return in 30 states, and in none of the others (including California, New York, Massachusetts, Virginia and D.C., which aren't on the list) [criminal list, rev. 1/14/2026]. Eviction records: not available in Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, South Dakota and Wyoming; limited in specific counties of 19 states [eviction list, rev. 8/12/2026] [county list]. TransUnion says coverage can vary both because of what each jurisdiction allows and because it holds back records that don't meet its data quality standards [source].

To see one state together with its fee rule and the packages that fit, use the tenant screening checker by state.

What to do if your state is missing

  • Criminal "No" state: SmartMove's list doesn't include your state, so a blank criminal section shouldn't be read as a clean record. Decide before you advertise how you'll handle criminal history, and check what your state lets you charge applicants on our application fee caps by state page.
  • Eviction "Not available" or a limited county: search the county or statewide court portal directly. Some states also restrict which eviction records may be used, covered on our eviction records page.
  • Phased jurisdiction: run the non-criminal bundle first, then the criminal report only after the applicant clears your other criteria [source]. The local rules are summarized on our phased screening page.
  • Comparing providers: our report comparison lists what each provider discloses about its own coverage gaps, and the "No criminal records are available" page explains a similar message on TurboTenant.

If you deny or change the terms for an applicant because of anything in the report, you owe them an adverse action notice.
