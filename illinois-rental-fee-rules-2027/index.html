Illinois Rental Fee Rules for 2027: the $50 Application Fee Limit and Fee Disclosure

Illinois added a new Section 35, "Rental fee transparency and limitations," to its Landlord and Tenant Act [P.A. 104-0479]. It caps what you can charge an applicant for screening, and it makes any non-optional fee that isn't on the first page of the lease uncollectible. Here is what the text says, section by section.

The short version. For covered Illinois leases, an application fee, including background checks, can't be more than $50. You can pass on a higher third-party background check cost only if you pay the provider first and bill the applicant, with the provider's receipts, within 14 days [765 ILCS 705/35(c)(1)]. Every non-optional fee must appear on the first page of the lease and in the listing [705/35(b)]. The Illinois Compiled Statutes list the section's source as P.A. 104-479, effective 1-1-27 [source].

When does the Illinois rental fee law start, and which leases does it cover?

The compiled statute shows the source as "P.A. 104-479, eff. 1-1-27" [source]. The section "applies to all lease agreements for residential rental property entered into after the effective date" of the Act [705/35(e)].

It does not apply to "lease agreements entered into for dwelling units in owner-occupied premises containing 6 units or fewer" [705/35(e)]. If you live in a building with 6 or fewer units and rent out the others, this section doesn't reach those leases.

How much can an Illinois landlord charge for an application or background check?

No landlord or lease may require "a fee for a rental application, including background checks, in excess of $50" [705/35(c)(1)]. A fee over $50 for a third-party background check is allowed only if all three of these are true [705/35(c)(1)(A)-(C)]:

  1. The actual cost of the third-party background check service is more than $50.
  2. You, the landlord, pay the upfront cost of the service.
  3. You bill the applicant within 14 days of the background check, with receipts from the provider.

If you don't send the bill and receipts within 14 days, "the fee for a third-party background check is waived." The fee also can't be the basis for an eviction action within the first year of the lease [705/35(c)(1)].

You also can't add a second fee at application time that duplicates screening costs or covers costs unrelated to screening [705/35(c)(2)]. Renaming a fee doesn't help: "A landlord may not rename a fee or charge to avoid application of this Section" [705/35(e)].

What if the applicant brings a reusable screening report?

Illinois already has a separate rule. If an applicant provides a reusable tenant screening report that was prepared within the previous 30 days by a consumer reporting agency, is available to you at no cost, and includes all the criteria you consistently use, you may not charge a fee to access it or an application screening fee [765 ILCS 705/30(b)(1)]. You may ask the applicant to state there has been no material change in the information [705/30(b)(2)]. See reusable screening reports by state.

What fees must be disclosed in the listing and the lease?

  • All non-optional fees, one-time or recurring, must be "explicitly contained on the first page of a lease agreement." If a fee isn't there, "a tenant shall not be liable for payment of such fee" [705/35(b)].
  • Non-optional fees must be disclosed "in a clear and conspicuous manner" in the listing or in a weblink that goes with it at the time of listing [705/35(b)(1)]. A "listing" is an ad or written notice that a property is for lease and includes the rental price [705/35(a)].
  • The lease disclosure or the listing must say whether utilities are included in the rent [705/35(b)(2)].

Which fees are banned outright?

Besides the application fee limit, no landlord or lease may require a fee or fine for [705/35(c)(3)-(11)]:

Can Chicago or other Illinois cities set stricter fee rules?

Yes, but not looser ones. Local governments "may regulate fees charged to tenants, but such regulations must at a minimum, restrict fees charged to tenants in a manner equal to this Section" [705/35(d)].

What happens if a landlord violates it?

Anyone alleging a violation may bring a civil action, and the court may order injunctive relief, monetary relief, attorney's fees and costs [705/35(f)].

Checklist for Illinois listings from January 1, 2027

  1. Set the application fee at $50 or less, or use the pay-first, bill-with-receipts-within-14-days route for a costlier third-party check.
  2. Put every non-optional fee on the first page of the lease.
  3. List non-optional fees in the ad (or a linked page) and say whether utilities are included.
  4. Drop any renewal, notice, after-hours, walk-through or routine-maintenance fees from your lease form.
  5. Hand applicants your written screening criteria so a reusable report can be checked against them.
