Tenant Screening Reports Compared: What Each One Actually Includes

Short answer: Published prices run from $14.95 (Rentec Direct Basic) to $55 (TurboTenant on its free plan). SmartMove costs $25 to $49 and the landlord or applicant can pay; RentRedi is $39.99, paid by the tenant. The bigger difference is coverage: SmartMove says its criminal records can be returned in only 30 states. [source][source][source][source][source]

Most comparisons stop at price. The bigger difference is what comes back, and in which states. DoorLoop figures are from its own pages as of November 25, 2026; the others are from each provider's own pricing or help page as of November 18, 2026, and each one links to it.

Does a "nationwide" report return the same records in every state?

A "nationwide criminal check" doesn't return the same records everywhere. State and local laws restrict what can be reported, and providers handle that differently. Here is what each one discloses.

SmartMove publishes its list

TransUnion SmartMove's disclaimer page says that, as of its January 14, 2026 revision, criminal records are available to return in 30 states: Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Maryland, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, New Mexico, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Washington, and Wisconsin [source].

The same page (revised August 12, 2026) says eviction records are not available in Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, South Dakota and Wyoming, and are limited in California, Connecticut, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, West Virginia and Wisconsin [source].

It also requires a phased screening (non-criminal bundle first, criminal only after the applicant meets preliminary criteria) in Ann Arbor, MI; Berkeley, CA; Cook County, IL; Detroit, MI; Maryland (statewide); New Jersey (statewide); New York City; Oakland, CA; and Washington, D.C. (revised October 1, 2026) [source].

TurboTenant describes it in its help center

TurboTenant says its criminal checks go through TransUnion and "cover all 50 states," but that reports are "subject to State Suppression Laws": look-back limits in states like Colorado and New York, and dismissed or expunged cases left out in places like Massachusetts and Hawaii [source]. In Cook County, IL; Detroit, MI; Montgomery County, MD; Oakland, CA; Berkeley, CA; and Washington, D.C., TurboTenant says results "will indicate whether records exist, but specific charge details will not be provided," because it does not support the two-phase process there [source]. See our explainer on "No criminal records are available".

RentRedi, Rentec Direct, RentPrep and DoorLoop

We did not find a state-by-state coverage list on the pages we reviewed for these four. DoorLoop says its screening reports are TransUnion-powered, including credit history, bankruptcies, eviction records and US national criminal background checks [source]. RentRedi's and RentPrep's credit products run on TransUnion (RentRedi says it partners with TransUnion [source]; RentPrep calls its credit-only product "the automated SmartMove package" [source]). We have not confirmed whether SmartMove's criminal-coverage limits also apply to their criminal searches, so treat that as an open question.

Which screening report should you choose?

  • Your rental is in a state missing from SmartMove's criminal list (for example California, New York, Massachusetts or Virginia): ask any provider, in writing, whether its criminal search returns records for your county before you charge an applicant. Our fee-cap table shows what you're allowed to charge.
  • You're in a phased-screening jurisdiction (Cook County, NYC, D.C., New Jersey, Maryland and the others listed above): you'll need a two-step workflow. SmartMove supports it; TurboTenant says it doesn't, and instead shows whether records exist without the charge details [SM][TT].
  • You want a person to review the background results: RentPrep lists an "FCRA-Certified Screener Review" in its background and complete packages [source].

If an applicant offers a report they already paid for, check whether your state makes you accept it or waive the fee: reusable reports by state.
