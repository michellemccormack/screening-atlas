Fair Chance Housing Ordinances: Where Landlords Can't Screen for Criminal History

"Criminal history is a protected class in some cities" isn't quite how the laws are written, but the effect can be close. A handful of cities bar landlords from using an applicant's criminal record at all, with narrow exceptions; others allow it only within set look-back periods or after an individual review. The rules are local, so the same screening report can be fine in one city and a violation across the county line.

The short version. Seattle still bars requiring disclosure of criminal history and taking adverse action based on it, but since June 6, 2023 no longer enforces its ban on asking [Seattle OCR]. Oakland generally bars asking about, requiring disclosure of, or using criminal history, with exemptions for some owner-occupied housing [City of Oakland]. Portland lets landlords choose low-barrier criteria (no denial for misdemeanors sentenced more than 3 years ago or felonies more than 7) or their own criteria with an individual assessment [Portland City Code 30.01.086(D)-(F)]. Cook County, D.C., New York City and New Jersey use phased screening; see phased screening.

Seattle: what the Ninth Circuit changed

Seattle's Fair Chance Housing Ordinance barred landlords from requiring disclosure of, or asking about, "any arrest record, conviction record, or criminal history" of current or prospective tenants, and from taking adverse action based on it [Yim v. City of Seattle, 9th Cir. 2023, citing S.M.C. 14.09.025(A)]. In Yim v. City of Seattle (filed March 21, 2023), the Ninth Circuit held that the inquiry provision violated landlords' First Amendment rights, rejected the landlords' challenge to the adverse action provision, and sent the case back to the district court on severability [Yim, summary].

The Seattle Office for Civil Rights says that as of June 6, 2023 it no longer enforces the ban on inquiring about criminal history, and continues to enforce the rest, including the bans (subject to exceptions) on requiring disclosure and on adverse action based on that history [Seattle OCR]. So in Seattle a landlord may ask, but generally can't deny or treat an applicant differently because of the answer.

Oakland: the notice and paperwork rules

  • No advertisement may say, directly or indirectly, that people with criminal history won't be considered or can't apply, except as required by state or federal law [O.M.C. 8.25.040(A)].
  • The city's notice of rights must appear prominently on application materials, websites and places applicants visit [8.25.040(C)]. The city publishes the notice [City of Oakland].
  • Any criminal history obtained must be kept for at least three years and kept confidential to the extent the law allows [8.25.040(D)].
  • If any adverse action is based on criminal history, the applicant gets a written notice with the reasons, how to complain to the city, local legal services, a copy of the record relied on, and a chance to respond before denial [8.25.030(D)].

Portland: two ways to screen

Portland requires every landlord to follow its general screening process, and then either use criteria no more prohibitive than the low-barrier criteria, or use the landlord's own criteria and conduct an individual assessment before denying [30.01.086(D)]. The individual assessment must consider any supplemental evidence the applicant provides, the nature and severity of the incidents, their number and type, the time elapsed and the applicant's age at the time [30.01.086(F)(1)]. A denial after an individual assessment needs a written notice within two weeks explaining the basis and why the supplemental evidence didn't make up for it [30.01.086(F)(2)]. Even under low-barrier criteria, the landlord must consider supplemental evidence submitted with the application before denying for criminal history [30.01.086(E)(2)(b)].

The same section caps income requirements at 2.5 times the rent, or 2 times for higher-rent units, depending on the Portland Housing Bureau's 80% median income rent figure [30.01.086(D)(2)]. Oregon's statewide rules are on our Oregon screening rules page.

What this means for your screening report

  • These ordinances control what you may do with a criminal report, and in Oakland whether you may ask for one at all for a covered unit.
  • In Portland, write your criteria down and decide in advance whether you're using the low-barrier criteria or doing individual assessments. See written screening criteria.
  • Old records have separate limits under the FCRA and some state laws. See how far back background checks go.
