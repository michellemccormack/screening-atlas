How to Get a Free Copy of Your Tenant Screening Report Before You Apply

Most renters know they can pull their credit reports. Fewer know that the companies landlords use for tenant screening keep their own files, with eviction cases, rent payment history and criminal records, and that you can ask to see them. Checking before you apply gives you time to dispute an error instead of finding it in a denial letter. This page is for applicants, and for landlords who want to point a rejected applicant to the right place.

The short version. A company that keeps files nationwide on "residential or tenant history" is a nationwide specialty consumer reporting agency [15 U.S.C. 1681a], and must give you your file disclosure free once in any 12-month period when you ask [1681j(a)(1)]. Separately, any reporting company that has a file on you must give you a free disclosure if you ask within 60 days after an adverse action notice [1681j(b)]. The CFPB lists 10 tenant screening companies with how to reach each one; all 10 are marked as offering a free report [CFPB list, Jan 2025].

When is a tenant screening report free?

  • Once every 12 months, on request, from each nationwide specialty consumer reporting agency. The statute applies the free annual disclosure to agencies that compile files nationwide on "residential or tenant history," among other categories [1681a] [1681j(a)(1)(A), (C)].
  • Within 60 days after an adverse action notice. If a landlord denied you, or approved you on worse terms, because of a report, the reporting company that has your file must give you the disclosure free if you request it within 60 days of getting the notice [1681j(b)]. The notice has to name the company (see our adverse action notice page).
  • Other cases: once in 12 months if you certify in writing that you're unemployed and plan to look for work within 60 days, receive public welfare assistance, or believe your file is inaccurate because of fraud [1681j(c)].

In California, if you paid a landlord a screening fee, the landlord must also give you a copy of the consumer credit report within seven days of receiving it [Civ. Code 1950.6(f)].

What should you do if the report is wrong?

SafeRent, RealPage, CIC and Experian RentBureau each list a dispute option on their consumer pages [SafeRent] [RealPage] [CIC] [Experian]. If an old eviction case shows up, check the court record yourself first; some states seal or limit eviction records (see eviction records and court portals).
