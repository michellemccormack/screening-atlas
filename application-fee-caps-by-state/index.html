Rental Application Fee Caps by State (2026)

What a landlord may charge someone who applies to rent, state by state, taken from the statute itself. We have reviewed 15 states so far; the rest are listed at the bottom as not yet reviewed. That doesn't mean they have no limit. We just haven't checked them yet.

The short version. Vermont bars application fees for residential rentals [source]. Maine, Rhode Island and Connecticut bar application or processing fees but let a landlord charge for a screening report, at actual cost in Maine and Rhode Island and up to $50 plus inflation in Connecticut [ME][RI][CT]. New York has the lowest dollar cap we found: actual cost or $20, whichever is less [NY]. Colorado, Oregon and Washington tie the fee to actual or customary cost and set no dollar figure.

State-by-state details

California: Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.6

Actual out-of-pocket cost, and no more than $30 per applicant, adjusted annually for CPI since Jan 1, 1998 [source]

unverified The current CPI-adjusted dollar amount is not stated in the statute; we have not confirmed a figure.

  • No fee if the landlord knows or should know no unit is available. [source]
  • Fee only if the landlord uses a first-come qualified-applicant process or refunds unselected applicants within the statute's deadlines. [source]
  • Itemized receipt required; applicant gets a copy of the credit report within 7 days. [source]
  • Landlord may accept a reusable screening report (§ 1950.1). [source]

Colorado: C.R.S. §§ 38-12-903, 38-12-904

No dollar cap; the whole fee must cover the landlord's actual (or average) cost of processing the application. Same fee for every applicant. [source]

  • No application fee if the applicant provides a portable tenant screening report (§ 38-12-904(1.5)), unless the landlord qualifies for the one-fee-at-a-time-and-refund exemption in (1.5)(f). [source]
  • Landlord must disclose anticipated costs or itemize actual costs, and give a receipt. [source]
  • Unused portion must be refunded (good-faith effort within 20 calendar days). [source]

Connecticut: Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-4d

No application processing/review fee. A tenant screening report fee of up to $50 plus a CPI-U adjustment set annually by the Commissioner of Housing (on and after Oct 1, 2023). [source]

unverified We have not confirmed the current CPI-adjusted amount.

  • Landlord must give the applicant a copy of the report (or how to get it) and the screening company's receipt or invoice. [source]
  • No move-in or move-out fees. [source]

Delaware: 25 Del. C. § 5514(d)

The greater of 10% of monthly rent or $50. [source]

  • Receipt required; landlord keeps records of application fees for at least 2 years. [source]
  • Charging more than allowed entitles the tenant to double the fee as damages. [source]

Maine: 14 M.R.S. § 6030-H

No application fee. Landlord may charge the actual cost of only one background check, credit check, or other screening process. [source]

  • No more than one screening fee per applicant in any 12-month period. [source]
  • Landlord must give the applicant a complete copy of the information obtained, and must tell the applicant this before charging. [source]

Maryland: Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-213

No flat cap. If non-deposit fees exceed $25, the landlord must return them except the portion actually spent on a credit check or other application expenses. [source]

  • Refund due within 15 days of occupancy or of a written decision that no tenancy will occur; otherwise liable for twice the fees. [source]
  • Does not apply to landlords offering 4 or fewer units on one parcel or at one location, or to seasonal or condominium rentals. [source]

Massachusetts: M.G.L. c. 186 § 15B(1)(b)

At or before the start of a tenancy a landlord may require only: first month's rent, last month's rent, a security deposit up to one month's rent, and the cost of a new lock and key. [source]

unverified The statute lists permitted charges rather than naming application fees; reading this as a bar on application fees is our interpretation.

Minnesota: Minn. Stat. § 504B.173

No dollar cap in this section. [source]

  • No fee when no unit is or will reasonably be available. [source]
  • Landlord may not cash or deposit the fee until prior applicants have been screened and rejected or have declined. [source]
  • Fee must be returned if the applicant is rejected for a reason not in the required disclosure, or if a prior applicant takes the unit; unused amounts are returned if no check is run. [source]

New York: N.Y. Real Prop. Law § 238-a(1)(b)

Background and credit check fees combined: the lesser of actual cost or $20. [source]

  • Fee must be waived if the applicant provides a background or credit check from the past 30 days. [source]
  • Landlord must give the applicant a copy of the check and the screening company's receipt or invoice. [source]

Oregon: ORS 90.295

No more than the landlord's average actual cost of screening, or the customary amount screening companies charge for comparable screening. [source]

  • Only one screening charge per applicant in any 60-day period, regardless of how many of the landlord's units they apply for. [source]
  • Written screening criteria and notice of the charge are required before accepting payment; receipt required. [source]

Rhode Island: R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-59

No rental application fee (effective Jan 1, 2024). Landlord may charge only the actual cost of an official state criminal background check and/or credit report. [source]

  • No charge if the applicant supplies a required background check or credit report issued within 90 days. [source]
  • Applicant who pays must get a copy of the report. [source]

Vermont: 9 V.S.A. § 4456a

No application fee for residential rentals. [source]

  • Landlord must accept any unexpired government ID, an ITIN, or an SSN for a background or credit check, and may not require an SSN. [source]

Virginia: Va. Code § 55.1-1203(C)

$50, plus the landlord's actual out-of-pocket cost paid to a third party for background, credit, or other checks. $32 (plus those costs) for public housing or HUD-regulated units. [source]

  • The Code shows this version as effective until July 1, 2027; another version follows. Re-check before that date. [source]

Washington: RCW 59.18.257

Screening costs only, and only after the required written disclosures. A landlord doing their own screening may charge actual costs, not more than what screening services customarily charge in the area. [source]

  • Landlord must say whether it accepts a comprehensive reusable tenant screening report. [source]

Wisconsin: Wis. Stat. § 704.085; Wis. Admin. Code ATCP 134.05(4)

Credit report: actual cost up to $25. Out-of-state applicants: background check at actual cost up to $25. [source]

  • No credit-check charge if the applicant provides a nationwide credit report less than 30 days old. [source]
  • Landlord must notify the applicant before ordering and give a copy of the report. [source]

Cities and counties can add rules

This page covers state law only. Some cities and counties have their own screening rules. Wisconsin's state extension office, for example, tells renters to "check local laws" because municipal codes can add protections [source]. Local rules will get their own pages.
