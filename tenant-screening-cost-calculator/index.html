Tenant Screening Cost Calculator: Landlord Pays vs. Applicant Pays

"Should I pay for screening or pass it to the applicant?" The answer is mostly arithmetic: how many people you screen per vacancy, how many vacancies you have a year, and which package you use. This calculator does that arithmetic with the prices each provider publishes. It runs in your browser and sends nothing anywhere.

Check the law first. Some states cap or ban applicant-paid screening fees, and some make you waive the fee when the applicant brings a recent report. See application fee caps by state and reusable reports by state. Some providers also decide who can pay (see the table below).

How to read the result

  • Landlord pays: your cost scales with how many people you screen. Screening only your top one or two applicants after a showing and an application review keeps it low.
  • Applicant pays: your cost is zero, but each applicant pays the full price, and the ones you turn down pay for nothing. Where a state caps the fee below the provider's price, the calculator flags it.
  • Platform-fixed pricing: on TurboTenant the applicant price depends on your plan ($55 on Free, $45 on Essentials/Pro) [source], so a paid plan lowers what applicants pay.
