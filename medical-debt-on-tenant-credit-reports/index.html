Medical Debt on a Rental Applicant's Credit Report: What Was Removed and Which States Ban It

An applicant tells you their score is low because of medical bills. Should that debt even be on the report? Since 2022 the three nationwide credit bureaus have dropped most medical collections, and several states now bar medical debt from credit reports altogether. Here's what changed, from the bureaus' own announcements and the state statutes.

The short version. Since July 1, 2022, paid medical collections are no longer on Equifax, Experian or TransUnion reports, and unpaid medical collections wait one year (up from six months) before they can appear [TransUnion]. Since April 11, 2023, medical collections with an initial balance under $500 are removed too [Equifax/Experian/TransUnion]. New York [GBL 380-j] and Minnesota [Minn. Stat. 332C.03] bar consumer reporting agencies from reporting medical debt. California bars anyone from furnishing medical debt to a credit reporting agency [Civ. Code 1785.27].

What the three bureaus removed

ChangeEffectiveSource
Medical collection debt "paid by the consumer in full" no longer included on U.S. consumer credit reportsJuly 1, 2022[TransUnion]
Waiting period before unpaid medical collection debt appears increased from six months to one yearJuly 1, 2022[TransUnion]
Medical collection debt with an initial reported balance under $500 removed; the bureaus said this took out nearly 70% of medical collection tradelinesApril 11, 2023[joint release]

These are the bureaus' own reporting policies, announced jointly by Equifax, Experian and TransUnion [source]. They don't cover an unpaid medical collection with an initial balance of $500 or more that is over a year old, so that can still appear unless a state law like those below applies.

"Consumer reporting agency" in the New York and Minnesota rules covers more than the three bureaus; Minnesota uses the federal FCRA definition [332C.03(c)]. This page covers the three states above, reviewed from official sources; other states' laws aren't covered here.

Where tenant screening credit reports come from

The most common small-landlord screening services pull TransUnion credit data: TurboTenant's screening report is "third-party information from TransUnion" [source], RentRedi's standard screening runs through TransUnion [source], and SmartMoveaffiliate link is TransUnion's own service. So the bureau-wide removals above apply to the credit portion of those reports. If an applicant says a medical collection on their report shouldn't be there, they can dispute it with the reporting agency; the dispute rights you must tell them about are on our adverse action notice page.
