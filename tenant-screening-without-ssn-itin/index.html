Screening an Applicant Without a Social Security Number: Which Services Accept an ITIN

New arrivals, international students and many long-time taxpayers apply with an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) or no U.S. number at all. Whether you can get a credit, criminal and eviction report on them depends on the screening service, even among services that all use TransUnion. And in some states, how you ask for ID is regulated too.

The short version. SmartMove can only process applicants "who have a valid U.S. Social Security number" [SmartMove]. TurboTenant accepts an ITIN; with neither number, TransUnion verification can't be completed [TurboTenant]. RentRedi has applicants enter the ITIN in the SSN field and then verify identity by phone with TransUnion [RentRedi]. Rentec Direct says its nationwide databases need an SSN and other numbers give inaccurate or no results [Rentec]. Oregon lists IDs a landlord must accept, including an ITIN card or any passport regardless of expiration [ORS 90.306].

State rules on ID and immigration status

StateRule
OregonA landlord may not ask about the immigration or citizenship status of an applicant, tenant or household member. If you require ID, you must accept any of these (or a combination) to verify name, date of birth and appearance: a Social Security card or evidence of an SSN; a certified birth record; a permanent resident card; an immigrant or nonimmigrant visa; an IRS ITIN card; "a passport, driver license or other government-issued identification, regardless of expiration date"; or nongovernment ID that permits reasonable verification. Exception: requirements of a federal rent-subsidy or affordable-rent program [ORS 90.306]
CaliforniaA landlord may not inquire about, or require a statement about, the immigration or citizenship status of a tenant, prospective tenant or occupant [Civ. Code 1940.3(b)]. The law still allows requesting information or documentation needed to verify a prospective tenant's financial qualifications or identity, and compliance with federal program obligations [1940.3(c)]

Nationally, the Fair Housing Act makes it unlawful to refuse to rent, or otherwise make a dwelling unavailable, because of national origin [42 U.S.C. 3604(a)]. Because some services can't process an applicant without an SSN, decide in advance what you'll accept instead and apply it the same way to every applicant who can't run the report, then put it in your written screening criteria.
