Can a Landlord Report Unpaid Rent to the Credit Bureaus? Furnisher Duties and How It Works

A tenant moves out owing two months' rent. Small claims feels like a lot of work, and collection agencies take a cut. Services now let individual landlords put that balance on the former tenant's credit report. Once you do, federal law treats you as a "furnisher" with duties of your own. Here is what the law says, and how one service describes its process.

The short version. Anyone who furnishes information to a credit bureau must not report information they know or have reasonable cause to believe is inaccurate, must correct what turns out to be wrong, and must flag information the consumer disputes [15 U.S.C. 1681s-2(a)]. When a bureau forwards a dispute, the furnisher must investigate and report back [1681s-2(b)]. Collection accounts generally can't appear on a consumer report after seven years [1681c(a)(4)].

Is it legal for a landlord to report unpaid rent to the credit bureaus?

Federal law doesn't bar it; it sets rules for anyone who furnishes information about a consumer to a consumer reporting agency [1681s-2]. Most individual landlords don't have direct accounts with the bureaus, so they go through a reporting service. FrontLobby, for example, says it reports to Equifax, Experian, TransUnion and the Landlord Credit Bureau [FrontLobby].

What does the FCRA require of a landlord who reports a debt?

The statute's separate written-notice rule for "negative information" applies to a "financial institution that extends credit" [1681s-2(a)(7)].

How long can unpaid rent stay on a tenant's credit report?

A consumer report may not include accounts placed for collection or charged to profit and loss that are more than seven years old [1681c(a)(4)]. The seven years start 180 days after the delinquency that came before the collection activity began [1681c(c)(1)]. FrontLobby says rental debt "can remain on a Tenant's credit file for up to seven years" [FrontLobby].

Does California require notice before reporting?

California's Consumer Credit Reporting Agencies Act says a "creditor" may submit negative credit information to a credit reporting agency "only if the creditor notifies the consumer affected." The notice must be in writing, delivered in person or mailed first class to the last known address, before or within 30 days after the information is sent [Cal. Civ. 1785.26(b),(c)]. The statute gives sample wording: "As required by law, you are hereby notified that a negative credit report reflecting on your credit record may be submitted to a credit reporting agency if you fail to fulfill the terms of your credit obligations" [1785.26(c)(2)].

Before you report a former tenant's balance

  1. Make sure the amount matches the lease and your ledger. Reporting a figure you have reason to believe is wrong is barred by 1681s-2(a)(1)(A) [source].
  2. Apply any security deposit you kept, so the balance you report is the amount actually owed.
  3. Give the tenant an address for disputes, and if they dispute the balance, make sure it is reported as disputed [1681s-2(a)(3)].
  4. Update the balance as payments come in, and correct it if you find an error [1681s-2(a)(2)].
  5. In California, send the written negative-information notice [1785.26].
