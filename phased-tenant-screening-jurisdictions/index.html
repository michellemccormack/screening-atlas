Phased (Two-Step) Tenant Screening: Cook County, D.C., New York City and New Jersey

"Phased screening" means the criminal check comes last: first income, credit, rental history and the rest, and only then, for an applicant who passes, a criminal background check that is limited to certain records. Screening providers build their products around it. Here is what four of these laws actually require, quoted from the law or the enforcing agency.

The short version. Cook County: prequalify on everything except criminal history, then check; consider only convictions from the last 3 years [Cook]. D.C.: no criminal questions until a conditional offer; afterwards only listed offenses from the last 7 years [D.C.]. New York City: written conditional offer first; then only sex-offense registrations, felonies from the last 5 years and misdemeanors from the last 3 [NYC]. New Jersey: conditional offer first; then only listed serious offenses plus indictable offenses within 6, 4 or 1 years depending on degree [NJ].

Details by jurisdiction

Cook County, Illinois (Just Housing Amendment)

  • Applications may not ask whether the applicant has a criminal record, and ads may not say things like "no felons" or "no criminal history" [source].
  • Before taking an application fee, the landlord must give its tenant selection criteria and a notice of the applicant's right to dispute and offer evidence of rehabilitation, among other items [source].
  • The three steps: prequalification (no criminal check allowed), then criminal background check and individualized assessment, then approval or denial with the right to dispute [source].
  • Exceptions: current registered sex offenders and current child sex offenders under residency restrictions [source].
  • The landlord does not have to hold the unit off the market during a dispute, and the law doesn't require landlords to run criminal checks at all [source].

Washington, D.C. (Fair Criminal Record Screening for Housing Act)

  • Before accepting an application fee, the provider must disclose in writing its eligibility criteria (financial, employment, criminal and rental history) and that the applicant may provide evidence of inaccuracies, rehabilitation or other mitigating factors [source].
  • The applicant can sign all authorizations up front; the provider just can't ask about or consider criminal accusations or convictions until after the conditional offer [source].
  • A "conditional offer" is one contingent on the later criminal-record inquiry or on any other eligibility criteria [source].
  • A withdrawal needs written, specific reasons and notice of the right to file a complaint with the D.C. Office of Human Rights [source].

New York City (Fair Chance for Housing Law)

  • In effect since January 1, 2025, and it covers current tenants (lease renewals) as well as applicants [source].
  • A conditional offer is "a written lease, rental agreement, or agreement for a sale that conditionally commits a unit" and can be revoked only in limited circumstances [source].
  • Never considered: sealed or expunged convictions, violations (non-criminal offenses), youthful offender adjudications, ACDs, and some out-of-state convictions, among others listed in the city notice [source].
  • Ads and applications may not make statements about criminal background checks or limits based on criminal records [source].

New Jersey (Fair Chance in Housing Act)

  • Before a conditional offer, the only criminal records a provider may consider are a conviction for manufacturing methamphetamine on the premises of federally assisted housing and lifetime sex-offender registration [source].
  • For offenses with a prison sentence, the 6/4/1-year clock runs from when the sentence concluded [source].
  • A provider using a screening vendor must take reasonable steps to make sure the vendor's check follows the Act [source].

Other places providers flag

SmartMove requires its phased workflow (a non-criminal bundle first, the criminal bundle only after you certify the applicant met your preliminary criteria) in Ann Arbor, MI; Berkeley, CA; Cook County, IL; Detroit, MI; Maryland (statewide); New Jersey (statewide); New York City; Oakland, CA; and Washington, D.C. (revised October 1, 2026) [source]. TurboTenant names Cook County, Detroit, Montgomery County MD, Oakland, Berkeley and D.C. as places where it shows only whether records exist, without charge details, because it doesn't support the two-phase process there [source].

We have not yet reviewed the local laws for Ann Arbor, Berkeley, Detroit, Oakland, Maryland or Montgomery County. Some of them may restrict criminal history more than a two-step process does, so don't treat them as identical to the four above.

Doing it with a screening service

  • Run credit, eviction, income and identity first; record your decision that the applicant passed (and, in D.C., NYC and NJ, make the conditional offer in writing) before ordering the criminal piece.
  • Make sure the report is filtered to the look-back window your jurisdiction allows. New Jersey makes you responsible for your vendor's compliance [source].
