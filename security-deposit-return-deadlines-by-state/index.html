Security Deposit Return Deadlines by State, and When the Clock Starts

"Deadlines run from 14 to 60 days depending on the state" is the usual answer, but the number of days is only half of it. The other half is the day the count begins: the end of the lease, the day the keys come back, the day the tenant sends a forwarding address, or some mix of those. Miss it and several states take away the landlord's right to keep anything.

The short version. Florida: 15 days to return the deposit if there's no claim, or 30 days to send a certified-mail or e-mail notice of a claim [Fla. Stat. 83.49(3)(a)]. Arizona: 14 days, excluding weekends and holidays, after the tenancy ends, possession is delivered and the tenant makes a demand [A.R.S. 33-1321(D)]. Virginia: 45 days [Va. Code 55.1-1226(A)]. Michigan: 30 days [MCL 554.609]. Minnesota: three weeks [Minn. Stat. 504B.178 subd. 3]. Ohio: 30 days [ORC 5321.16(B)]. Wisconsin: 21 days [Wis. Adm. Code ATCP 134.06(2)].

Does the tenant have to give a forwarding address?

It matters in several of these states. In Minnesota the three-week clock runs only after the landlord receives the tenant's mailing address or delivery instructions [Minn. 504B.178 subd. 3(a)]. Ohio requires the tenant to give a forwarding address in writing, and a tenant who doesn't loses the right to the statute's damages and attorney fees [ORC 5321.16(B)]. Arizona's 14 days start only after the tenant demands the deposit, and the landlord mails the list to the last known address unless other arrangements are made in writing [A.R.S. 33-1321(D)]. In Florida, a tenant who leaves without at least 7 days' written notice that includes a forwarding address relieves the landlord of the 15/30-day notice requirement, though not of the tenant's right to the deposit [Fla. Stat. 83.49(5)].

Virginia says what happens when there's no address at all: the landlord may keep holding the deposit in escrow, and one year after the 45-day period ends may remit it to the State Treasurer as unclaimed property [Va. 55.1-1226(B)].

Can the tenant object to the deductions?

Some states build an objection window into the process:

  • Florida. The tenant has 15 days after receiving the notice to object in writing; if they don't, the landlord may deduct the claim and must send the balance within 30 days after the date of the notice [83.49(3)(b)]. The statute sets out the wording of the notice [83.49(3)(a)].
  • Michigan. The notice of damages must say, in 12-point bold type, that the tenant must respond by mail within 7 days or forfeit the amount claimed [554.609(4)].
  • Arizona. If the tenant doesn't dispute the itemized list within 60 days after it's mailed, it is deemed valid and final [33-1321(D)].

What if the damage is more than the deposit?

A deposit deadline doesn't end the landlord's claim for larger damage. Virginia gives the landlord an extra 15 days to itemize when damage exceeds the deposit and needs a third-party contractor, if the landlord says so within the 45 days [55.1-1226(E)]. Florida lets a landlord who missed the notice deadline still sue for damages after returning the deposit [83.49(3)(a)]. See damage beyond the security deposit for small claims limits.

What landlords should do

  • Put the move-out date and your state's deadline on the calendar the day the tenant gives notice.
  • Ask for a forwarding address in writing at move-out, and keep it.
  • Use the delivery method the statute names: certified mail or e-mail in Florida [83.49(3)(a)], first-class mail in Arizona unless agreed otherwise in writing [33-1321(D)].
  • Document the unit's condition at move-in, so deductions can be tied to it. See move-in checklist requirements by state.
