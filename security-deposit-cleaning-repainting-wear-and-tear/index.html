Can a Landlord Deduct Carpet Cleaning and Repainting From a Security Deposit?

A tenant moves out after three years and the landlord keeps $600 for professional carpet cleaning and a full repaint. Is that allowed? In every state reviewed here, the answer starts with the same rule: a landlord can't charge the deposit for ordinary wear and tear. The details on cleaning, paperwork and deadlines differ a lot.

The short version. California bars claims for "ordinary wear and tear or the effects thereof," including "the cumulative effects of ordinary wear and tear" over one or more tenancies, and bars charging for professional carpet cleaning unless it's reasonably necessary to restore the move-in condition [Cal. Civ. 1950.5(e)(2)]. Washington bars withholding for wear from ordinary use, and for carpet cleaning unless the landlord documents wear beyond ordinary use [RCW 59.18.280(1)(c)]. Oregon allows a carpet-cleaning deduction only under three conditions [ORS 90.300(7)(c)]. Massachusetts allows deductions for damage, "reasonable wear and tear excluded" [c.186 s.15B(4)].

Can a landlord charge for repainting after a long tenancy?

Not for paint that has simply aged. In California, the landlord can't claim for ordinary wear and tear "whether the wear and tear preexisted the tenancy or occurred during the tenancy," or for its cumulative effects [Cal. Civ. 1950.5(e)(2)(A)], and charges for work and materials are limited to a reasonable amount to restore the move-in condition, "exclusive of ordinary wear and tear" [1950.5(e)(2)(B)]. Washington and Oregon also exclude ordinary wear [RCW 59.18.280(1)(c)(i)] [ORS 90.300(7)(a)(B)]. Damage beyond normal wear, such as holes or stains the tenant caused, is a different matter in each of these states.

Washington adds a limit for partial damage: a landlord can't withhold more than the cost to repair or replace the damaged portion when the damage "does not encompass the item's entirety" [RCW 59.18.280(1)(c)(iv)].

Can the tenant fix problems before moving out?

In California, the landlord must tell the tenant in writing that they can ask for an initial inspection, no earlier than two weeks before move-out, and then give an itemized list of proposed deductions so the tenant can fix them [Cal. Civ. 1950.5(f)]. The New York AG guide says tenants can ask for a pre-move-out inspection and must be allowed to be present [NY AG guide].

What landlords should do at move-in

  • Document the condition. Massachusetts ties deductions to the statement of condition [s.15B(4)], Washington to the written checklist [RCW 59.18.280(1)(c)(iii)], and California now requires photos [1950.5(g)].
  • In Oregon, put any carpet-cleaning deduction in the written rental agreement [ORS 90.300(7)(c)(A)(iii)].
  • Keep receipts and invoices for every deduction.
