Bank-Linked Income Verification: What the Landlord Actually Sees

Applicants are increasingly asked to log in to their bank or payroll account through a service like Plaid during a rental application, and the obvious question is what the landlord gets to see afterward. The answer depends on the screening provider. Here is what three of them say on their own pages.

The short version. RentRedi's Plaid report shows an income summary, earnings by month, income sources, an asset summary and the linked accounts, and RentRedi says landlords get these insights "without accessing full bank statements" [RentRedi]. TurboTenant's Snappt result shows a pass/fail ruling and, when income is calculated, approximate monthly and annual gross income, employer, employment status and type, and pay-period breakdowns [TurboTenant]. SmartMove sells income verification as an $11 add-on covering payroll, gig, bank, benefits and uploaded documents [SmartMove].

Things worth knowing on each platform

RentRedi (Plaid)

The landlord turns Income & Asset Verification on per unit under Leasing → Screening Rules [source]. RentRedi says that, based on the verification method used, Plaid "can identify income sources and provide information about recent and historical income" [source]. If an applicant has no bank account or their bank isn't supported, they can decline the step and explain why [source].

TurboTenant (Snappt)

TurboTenant tells landlords to verify income independently because applicants self-report it on the application [source]. On Pro, Snappt reviews the documents; "Insufficient" means Snappt couldn't evaluate the document (wrong type or format), not that fraud was found [source]. A Fraud flag is "not an automatic rejection," and a Verified result "does not mean TurboTenant has approved the applicant" [source].

SmartMove

Don't confuse SmartMove's two income products. Income Verification is the $11 add-on that checks payroll, bank and other sources [source]. Income Estimator, included in SmartCheck Premium [source], doesn't connect to any account: it compares the applicant's self-reported income with a TransUnion model based on credit-report data and tells you whether more verification is recommended [source]. SmartMove says income verification is processed separately and shouldn't delay the credit, criminal or eviction reports [source].

If you deny someone because of the income result

If anything in a screening report, including an income result delivered with it, contributes to a denial or a condition like a co-signer, see what you owe the applicant on our adverse action notice page.

DoorLoop

DoorLoopaffiliate link says it can "verify applicant income directly from bank or payroll providers instead of relying on uploaded pay stubs alone" [source]. Its help center lists income verification as a $10 add-on to a $35 TransUnion screening, paid by the applicant [source]. The pages we reviewed do not list the fields the landlord sees, so we have not added DoorLoop to the table above.
